## 4. The short-epoch lever — now with the washout modelled

v1 published a short-epoch table and **warned that it did not model the
washout**. Modelling it: the washout is fixed at 2 h, so the useful fraction of
an `H`-hour epoch is `(H−2)/H`, and both exposure quantities scale by `(H−2)/22`
rather than `H/24`.

| epoch | useful fraction | MDE 20% | MDE 25% | MDE 30% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **24 h** | 92% | 242 ep = **242 d** | 152 ep = 152 d | 102 ep = 102 d |
| **12 h** | 83% | 286 ep = **143 d** | 178 ep = 89 d | 122 ep = 61 d |
| **8 h** | 75% | 340 ep = **113 d** | 212 ep = 71 d | 144 ep = 48 d |
| **6 h** | 67% | 408 ep = **102 d** | 254 ep = 64 d | 172 ep = 43 d |
| **4 h** | 50% | 612 ep = **102 d** | 382 ep = 64 d | 258 ep = 43 d |

**The lever saturates at ~6 h.** Between 6 h and 4 h the calendar stops
improving — the washout starts eating half of each epoch and consumes exactly the
gain from fitting more epochs into a day. v1's table, without the washout, was
monotonic and would have suggested shortening further.

What this table **still does not** model: the carry-over of Appendix B.
Shortening the epoch brings arm switches closer together, and the premise that
2 h suffices to wash the effect out was calibrated for 24 h epochs. **A 2 h
washout in a 6 h epoch is a stronger claim than in a 24 h one, and it has not
been verified.** Before adopting short epochs, that needs its own evidence —
otherwise a calendar problem is traded for an internal-validity one.

## 5. The ICC confidence interval — now official

The mean squares come from the same code that produces the point estimate
(`icc_anova` returns `ms_between`, `ms_within`, `F`, d.f. and the CI). v1 had to
reconstruct the ANOVA in a separate script, which gave 0.0964 against the
canonical 0.1175 and forced the document to say the interval indicated "the
width, not the official interval". That divergence no longer exists.

| | |
|---|---|
| ICC (point) | **0.0985** |
| 95% CI (Searle, one-way) | **[0.0570 ; 0.1814]** |
| F | 7.112 (d.f. 26 / 1,484) |
| `MS_between` / `MS_within` | 12,122.06 / 1,698.74 |
| m̄ | 55.96 |

Validation: `tests/test_icc_ci.py` confronts the pure-stdlib F implementation
against `scipy` at 150 points (CDF and quantile, d.f. from (1.1) to (500,500)) —
agreement to 1e-10 on the CDF. **`scipy` stays in the test, never in the
canonical path**: the script is pre-registered and a third party must be able to
run it without installing anything. The test skips (saying so) if `scipy` is
absent.

### The imbalance caveat — checked, and it did not hold

Searle's CI assumes balanced clusters and ours are not (27 clusters in the ICC,
sizes from 1 to ~100, two partial epochs, m̄ = 55.96). The expectation was that
it would come out **anticonservative** — too narrow. I ran the cluster bootstrap
(`icc_bootstrap.py`, 5,000 resamples of whole epochs with replacement, public
seed from round 31309420) to check:

| | lower | upper | width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Searle | 0.0570 | **0.1814** | 0.1244 |
| Percentile bootstrap | 0.0405 | 0.1561 | 0.1155 |

**The bootstrap is 7% narrower, not wider** — the caveat did not materialise. And
on the side that decides the sizing, Searle is the **more conservative of the
two**: upper limit 0.1814 against 0.1561, which at MDE 20% is worth 410 days
against 358.

So **the published CI remains Searle's**, and it errs on the safe side. Swapping
it for the bootstrap would shorten the study by 52 days on the basis of an
analysis that was not pre-specified — exactly the kind of trade a
pre-registration exists to prevent.

⚠️ Two caveats about the bootstrap itself: it is a **simple percentile**
bootstrap, and for a statistic bounded in [0.1] and skewed like the ICC the
percentile method is known to be biased (BCa would correct it); and resampling 27
clusters with replacement leaves only ~63% distinct clusters per replicate, which
should widen the interval, not narrow it. Both point to treating the bootstrap as
a **check**, not a substitute.

**A bug this exercise caught:** the first version of the bootstrap computed
`span_por_sessao` over the *selected* episodes rather than over all of them, and
reproduced an ICC of 0.1204 against the canonical 0.0985. A session's duration is
a fact about the session, not about the sample; restricting the span shrinks the
density's denominator and inflates the ICC. A CI built on that point would not be
the CI of the published estimator. Requiring the script to reproduce the point
**before** looking at the interval is what exposed this.

### What the interval does to the calendar

| ICC | MDE 20% | MDE 25% | MDE 30% |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI lower 0.057 | **158 d** | 98 d | 68 d |
| point 0.098 | **242 d** | 152 d | 102 d |
| CI upper 0.181 | **410 d** | 256 d | 174 d |

**The uncertainty is worth 252 days at MDE 20%** — more than the point estimate
itself. Sizing on the point is a bet; sizing on the upper limit is what the
`feedback_estimator_choice_inside_the_noise` note already mandated.

At the upper limit with an 8 h epoch: MDE 20% = 167 d · MDE 25% = 104 d ·
MDE 30% = 71 d. That is the combination that survives the ICC's worst case —
subject to the carry-over caveat of §4.

## 6. What remains open

1. **4 extension-2 episodes without a 3rd verdict** (2.1% of 187). The cause is
   now **known**: `served: glm-5.3`, `stop: max_tokens`, `blocos: ['thinking']`,
   `output_tokens` hitting the ceiling — the model spends its entire budget
   thinking and emits no text. It is not episode size (`input_tokens` from 384 to
   2,336). The retry now doubles the budget when it detects that signature, and
   that closed 2 of 6. **I deliberately stopped at the remaining 4:** §4.1 locks
   "one resend, then count as absent", and escalating beyond that would mean
   loosening a pre-registered rule to obtain more data.
2. **The panel cannot prove it judged with glm-5.2** on the earlier verdicts.
   `model_served` is recorded from now on (`INCIDENTS.md#2026-08-14`), but not
   retroactively: the 3,348 `zhipu` verdicts already collected remain without
   proof.
3. **Two corpus epochs are partial** — 08-11 (83.5%) and 08-14 — through
   right-censoring in extraction. Declared before adjudication in
   `EXTENSION-2-SEED-2026-08-14.md`.
4. **Carry-over for short epochs** (§4): the 2 h washout was calibrated for 24 h
   epochs. A prerequisite for adopting the lever.
